AsmFEA - Getting started

Stress analysis of a whole SOLIDWORKS assembly, from a task pane, in a few clicks.

1Find AsmFEA in SOLIDWORKS

  1. Start SOLIDWORKS (2018 or later; one of the versions you ticked in the installer). AsmFEA loads with it.
  2. On the right of the window, the task pane has a new tab with the coloured hexagon icon (the one at the top of this page): click it to open AsmFEA.
  3. If it is missing: Tools > Add-ins, tick AsmFEA in both columns, then OK.

2Prepare the assembly and start a study

  • Open the assembly and give every part a material (right-click the part > Material). A single part works too: its bodies are treated as the parts, and bodies that touch are joined.
  • Click New in the pane and name the study. It is saved inside the assembly (or part). Every setting is kept as you make it.
  • The pane has five tabs, used in order: Loads, Connections, Run, Results and Fatigue. The main button on each tab is the dark blue one; red buttons remove something.
Parts that touch are joined automatically. Mates tell AsmFEA where parts can slide or turn; weld beads are used too, and a sub-assembly is treated as one rigid piece.

3Add fixtures and loads

  1. Select one or more faces on the model.
  2. On the Loads tab, under Add to the selected faces, click Fixed (held still) or Pinned (can rotate).
  3. Loads belong to a load case (a set of loads solved together). Pick one at the top of the tab, or click New load case; every case is solved in the same run. Fixtures apply to all cases. Tick Include buckling in load case to check a case for buckling too (runs take longer).
  4. Select faces and click Force or Pressure, or click Gravity for self-weight. Give the force as X, Y and Z components; the resultant shows as you type.
  5. For a force acting away from the faces (a lever arm, a torque), also give how far from the faces' centre it acts, in X, Y and Z (leave 0, 0, 0 for a force on the faces).
  6. For fluid in a tank, select the wetted faces and click Hydrostatic. Give the fluid's density (water 1000 kg/m³), the absolute pressure on its top (1 atmosphere, 0.101325 MPa, for an open tank; the air outside balances it), and where the top of the fluid is, in X, Y and Z from the faces' centre; a ball and triad marks it on the model. The pressure rises with depth below it; faces above it are not loaded.

Click a row in the list to highlight its faces on the model; a load also shows an arrow pointing at where it acts. Double-click a row to change it.

4Check how the parts connect

  • Click Analyse connections: the list shows every place where parts meet and how they will be joined.
  • Click a connection to see it on the model: red balls mark the faces that join, and the view zooms to it. The Auto zoom slider sets how close: far left shows the whole model, far right the connection's faces.
  • To change one, select it and click Edit..., or use Set the selected connections to: Fixed (bonded), Sliding with friction..., Sliding joint... or No separation (sliding, never parting). Remove disconnects the parts there; Automatic undoes your choice.
  • From the SolidWorks selection: Add hinge... makes a pin joint on a hole or pin face, Join connects two parts that do not touch, and Rigid... treats parts whose shape is only approximate as rigid (they carry load and weight, but are not meshed).
  • Weld beads appear as their own rows.
  • On a large model (over 50 parts) every part is first bonded to the parts it touches, and mates are applied where they free a motion that matters; small parts hanging off the structure are left out and listed in the report. Advanced > Connections on the Run tab chooses the method yourself.
  • If parts are still floating, a window lists them with the gap to their nearest neighbour: raise the bond tolerance to join them, or leave them out of the analysis (they stay in SolidWorks).
Hover over any button for a tip. Double-click a connection or a warning, or press ?, for a full explanation.

5Run

  • On the Run tab click Run. The bar and the line below it show what is happening; a first run takes from under a minute to a few minutes. The bar is green while the results match the study as it is, and red when something has changed since.
  • Advanced (click to open) sets what to Optimise for: Speed (a coarse mesh, one solve: quick, for comparing designs), Balanced (for most work) or Accuracy (a finer mesh for local stresses, and parts whose results have not settled solved again finer). AsmFEA decides the rest from this and your computer's free memory and cores, always leaving room for SolidWorks and other programs; the plan shows under the buttons. You can also set the mesh and solver by hand there, and Fast load-size changes: each load solved once, so changing only load sizes afterwards needs no new solve.
  • Pass/fail criteria (click to open) holds the limits the checks and reports use: maximum deflection and minimum factors of safety. Changing a limit re-checks the last results without solving again.
  • Check only just checks the set-up, quickly, without solving.
  • Fast run is for trying design changes: a coarser model that shows where the problem is and whether a change helped, in a fraction of the time. Its results are marked APPROXIMATE and it writes no report: do a full Run at the end, before you rely on the numbers.
  • A re-run redoes only what your changes need: with nothing changed it finishes in seconds, and a new pass/fail limit skips the solve. Tick Full re-run to redo everything.
  • A model too large for CalculiX to fit in your computer's memory is no longer refused: AsmFEA solves it with its own hybrid solver, part by part, using the memory it has free. The report says when that happened.
  • A run is never stopped for memory. If other programs take memory while it runs, a box says the solve is continuing more slowly; closing programs you don't need speeds it up again.
  • The results say how far to trust them: a coloured line on the Results tab, and the reports, grade them High, Good or Indicative with the likely error on displacements and stresses, from how the model was solved. Click the line to see why.
  • Anything that needs attention is listed: double-click it to see what it means and what to do. An error stops the run; a warning lets it finish but may affect the results, and always says what you can do about it (tick Show warnings to list them). What AsmFEA dealt with itself is not listed.

6Look at the results

  • The Results tab shows the pass/fail checks and the key numbers for each load case. Stress is judged as an engineer would: through the wall (membrane plus bending), round joints as well, and not on the spikes at sharp edges. The factor of safety is given on yield and, for information, on ultimate strength.
  • Stress, Displacement and FoS open a 3-D window (their tooltips repeat what follows):
    • left drag to rotate, wheel to zoom, right drag to pan, as in SOLIDWORKS; Reset view (or R) goes back;
    • click the model to see the part and its stress, displacement and FoS at that point; click the background to clear it;
    • ▶ play ramps the load up and down so you can watch the stress build; click it again to stop at the full load;
    • Deformation x exaggerates the deformed shape, on every plot (0 shows it undeformed), and Undeformed shape adds a grey outline of the original;
    • Min / max shows the highest and lowest values; drag their labels anywhere;
    • right-click a part to see it as an outline and look at the parts behind it; right-click it again, or the background, to bring it back;
    • local peaks beyond the assessed stress show magenta; grey parts (such as bushes) are not assessed: check their bearing pressure by hand.
  • Open report gives the Word report, Open summary a half-page summary, and Open data the data behind them (the model, materials, connections, forces and detailed results). They need a full run (they are greyed out after a fast run).
  • The pictures follow the way you look at the model in SOLIDWORKS: turn it, then open the results again.

7Fatigue

Fatigue is cracking under repeated loading, at stresses well below yield. It matters for anything loaded many times: lifting, travelling, vibrating or rotating parts, and even a rig that only has to last a few hours can see thousands of cycles. The Fatigue tab works it out from the static results, with no new solve.

  1. Choose the standard (BS 7608 by default) and how many times the spectrum repeats in the design life.
  2. Fill in the loading spectrum: each row is a load case cycling between two percentages of it (100 % to 0 %: the load put on and taken off) a number of times. Use one load case at both ends, unless the load moves between two positions.
  3. The S-N data per material is built in: the SolidWorks material's own curve if it has one, otherwise published data, or an estimate from the tensile strength (marked ESTIMATED). Give your own if you have it.
  4. Click Assess fatigue. The result shows the damage over the design life (1 means a crack is expected) and the fatigue factor: how much every stress could rise before that. Run does not assess fatigue; after a new run the bar turns red until you click Assess fatigue again.
  5. Bonded joints (welds) are listed weakest first, with the factors of the weld and of the wall under it: click one to see it on the model.
  6. Fatigue report, Summary and Data open its own documents, separate from the static report. Hover over any setting for an explanation.

8Optional: PrePoMax, for a closer look

PrePoMax is a free program for exploring results in detail: section cuts, values at any point, animations. AsmFEA does not need it, but Open in PrePoMax on the Results tab opens the last run in it.

  1. Download it from prepomax.fs.um.si/downloads (version 2.6.0 is the one AsmFEA was tested with).
  2. It is a zip file: unzip it anywhere, for example C:\PrePoMax. There is nothing to install.
  3. In AsmFEA click Open in PrePoMax and choose Find PrePoMax.exe... the first time; AsmFEA remembers it.

✓How accurate is AsmFEA?

The verification report (also in the Start menu, and as a PDF in the AsmFEA folder) compares AsmFEA with classical hand calculations on four test cases (a cantilever, a beam under its own weight, two members joined end to end, and a buckling column) and checks the CalculiX solver against its own test examples and two NAFEMS benchmarks. Every comparison came within its acceptance criterion, the largest difference being 0.6 %.

Satisfy yourself of its accuracy for your own work by running your own tests against hand calculations or other trusted results before relying on it.

9Keep your work

  • The study is saved inside the assembly: save the assembly to keep it.
  • Results and reports (static and fatigue) are in a folder next to the assembly called <assembly name>_FEA.
  • Units are millimetres, newtons and MPa throughout.
Always check its results with engineering judgement and a hand calculation before relying on them for anything that matters.
AsmFEA uses CalculiX (solver) and Gmsh (mesher), free software downloaded by the installer; see the licenses folder.
